IDML Export — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 25 August 2026

IDML Export is a Figma plugin that converts a Figma frame into an Adobe InDesign .idml file — live text, named CMYK and spot swatches, layers, vectors and linked images. It works together with a free companion application, Monochrome bridge, that you install on your own Mac.

In one sentence: nothing about your design, your file, or your machine is ever sent to us or to any third-party server. Everything happens on your own computer.

Data collection

We do not collect, store, or receive any data about you or your work. We operate no server, no account system, no database, and no analytics of any kind.

  • No analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting, no cookies, no identifiers.
  • We never see your file, your designs, your text, your images, or your folder paths.
  • The plugin does not know who you are beyond what Figma's own Payments system requires in order to manage your subscription (see Payments below).

The companion app (Monochrome bridge)

This is the part that deserves the most detail, because it is the part that is unusual.

Why it exists. A Figma plugin runs in a sandbox that cannot write files to your disk or assemble an archive of the size a real IDML package requires. So the conversion is split: the plugin reads your design inside Figma and produces an intermediate description of it; the companion turns that into the .idml file and writes it to the folder you chose.

How the two talk. Over loopback only — http://127.0.0.1:7331, the address that means "this same computer". The companion binds exclusively to the loopback interface and additionally rejects any connection that does not originate from it. It is not reachable from your network, your office, or the internet, and it does not open any port to the outside.

What is sent to it. Only what is needed to build the file, and only from the plugin to the companion on your own machine:

  • The geometry, styling and structure of the frames you chose to export — positions, sizes, transforms, colours, strokes, effects, layer names.
  • The text content of those frames, including font names, because the export produces live, editable type rather than a picture of it.
  • Image bytes for images that are exported from Figma rather than linked from disk.
  • The folder paths you choose for output and for linked artwork, so the companion can write the .idml there and read the hi-res originals you asked it to link.

What it does with that. It writes the .idml file (and any exported images) into the output folder you selected, and returns the result to the plugin. That is all.

What it does not do. The companion makes no outbound network connections of its own. It does not phone home, check for updates, report usage, or transmit anything anywhere. Your design data reaches your disk and stops there.

Access control. Requests to the companion are authenticated with a token generated on your machine at install time, which you paste into the plugin once during setup. It is stored locally (see Local storage below) and never leaves your computer.

Lifecycle. The companion runs only when you open it. It stops listening after 60 minutes of inactivity, releasing the port; you start it again from its menu-bar icon. It never installs a background service and never starts itself — optionally, and only if you switch it on yourself, it can open at login the same way any other application does. It is signed and notarized by Apple.

It is yours to remove. The companion is an ordinary Mac application. Deleting it from /Applications removes it completely; the plugin then simply reports that no companion is available.

Network access

The plugin's Figma manifest declares exactly two allowed domains, and it contacts no others:

  1. http://localhost:7331 — the companion app on your own machine, described above. This traffic never leaves your computer.
  2. https://monochrome-downloads.github.io — a read-only fetch of a small static versions.json file, so the plugin can tell you when a newer companion build is available. This is an ordinary web request for a public file. It sends no information about you, your file, or your work — but, like any web request, GitHub (which hosts that page) will see that a request was made, in the same way it would if you opened the download page in a browser. We receive nothing from it.

The companion app itself, as stated above, makes no network requests at all — which is why the version check is done on the plugin side instead.

Local storage

Your settings are stored locally only, using Figma's own storage APIs. These are provided and controlled by Figma, on your machine — they are not a service we operate, and we have no access to them.

  • clientStorage (per-user, on your machine): your chosen output and links folder paths, your saved colour-mapping profiles and client names, your export preferences, and the companion access token.
  • pluginData (attached to the Figma file itself): per-object print settings such as overprint flags, so they travel with the design rather than with your machine.

Payments

Subscription state and checkout are handled entirely by Figma's own Payments platform (figma.payments), not by us. Figma — not this plugin — processes your payment method and billing details, under Figma's own privacy policy. We never see your payment information, and we receive no personal data from Figma's payments system; the plugin can see only whether the current user is paid or unpaid.

Third parties

There are none. IDML Export uses no third-party services, SDKs, analytics providers, or hosted APIs. It integrates only with Figma's own plugin APIs, and with the companion app on your own machine.

Children

This plugin is a professional design tool and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If this plugin's or the companion's data practices ever change — for example if a genuine network feature were ever added — this page will be updated first, with the date at the top revised, and the change will be reflected in the plugin's next Community listing update.
